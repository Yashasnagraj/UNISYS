Resonant Modal Spectroscopy 2.0: 
Multi-Modal Diagnostic Improvisation 
Executive Summary 
This report details the technical improvisation of the Resonant Modal Spectroscopy (RMS) 
device to expand its clinical utility beyond simple fluid detection. By upgrading the actuation 
system to a wideband Voice Coil Actuator (VCA) and implementing Mechanical Impedance 
Analysis (Z=F/v), the device can now address: 
1. Orthopedics: Monitoring long-bone fracture healing (Stiffness recovery). 
2. Pulmonology: Detecting Pneumothorax and Lung Compliance. 
3. Urology: Non-invasive Bladder Pressure/Volume sensing. 
1. Improvisation I: Orthopedic Fracture Monitoring 
X-rays show calcium, which takes weeks to appear. However, the mechanical stiffness of a 
fracture site returns much earlier as the soft callus hardens. This device tracks that mechanical 
return. 
1.1 The Physics: The "Broken Beam" Model 
● Intact Bone: High stiffness (k), acts as a rigid beam. High natural frequency (f_n > 250 
Hz). 
● Fractured Bone: Acts as two masses connected by a weak spring (the callus). The 
resonant frequency drops dramatically (f_n < 100 Hz). 
● Healing: As the callus mineralizes, the stiffness (E) increases, and the resonant 
frequency shifts back up. 
### 1.2 Protocol: Cross-Fracture Transmission Unlike the single-point fluid test, fracture 
detection uses a Pitch-Catch method. 
● Excitation: The device "pings" the distal bone (e.g., Ankle). 
● Sensing: The sensor is placed proximal to the fracture (e.g., Knee). 
● Metric: Tibial Stiffness Index (TSI). A rising TSI indicates healing. 
2. Improvisation II: Organ Compliance (Lungs & 
Bladder) 
Compliance is the inverse of stiffness (C = 1/k). Pathologies change the tension and stiffness of 
hollow organs. 
2.1 Pulmonary: Pneumothorax & "Digital Percussion" 
● Physics: A pneumothorax (collapsed lung) creates a free air pocket between the lung 
and chest wall. This air pocket resonates like a drum. 
● Signature: 
○ Normal: Damped, low-frequency thud. 
○ Pneumothorax: High-amplitude Hyper-Resonance in the 200–400 Hz range. 
● Improvisation: The device acts as a "Digital Pleximeter," quantifying the "hollowness" of 
the chest sound. 
2.2 Urology: Bladder Pressure/Stiffness 
● Physics: The bladder wall behaves like a tensioned membrane (Laplace's Law). As it fills, 
wall tension increases, and stiffness rises. 
● Method: Lamb Wave Analysis. The device excites the lower abdomen at low 
frequencies (20–50 Hz). 
● Metric: The speed of the wave (c_g) traveling across the bladder wall correlates with 
Intravesical Pressure, []. This allows non-invasive monitoring for neurogenic bladder 
patients. 
3. Critical Limitations & Engineering Solutions 
Implementing these new features introduces significant physical challenges. Below is the 
roadmap to overcome them. 
Limitation A: Soft Tissue Artifact (STA) 
The Problem: The "wobble" of skin and fat acts as a shock absorber. It dampens high 
frequencies (bone signals) and introduces false resonance peaks (fat resonance). 
● Impact: A "soft" reading might just be obesity, not a fracture. 
The Solution: The "Pre-Load" Protocol 
1. Hardware: Integrate a Load Cell (Force Sensor) behind the probe tip. 
2. Algorithm: The device only triggers the scan when the operator applies a specific force 
(e.g., 10 Newtons). 
○ Why: Compressing the soft tissue stiffens it ("bottoming out" the spring), effectively 
coupling the sensor directly to the bone/organ. 
3. Active Cancellation: Use a dual-sensor probe (skin sensor vs. deep sensor) and 
subtract the skin surface noise. 
Limitation B: Depth of Penetration 
The Problem: High frequencies (used for bone) do not penetrate deep into the abdomen to 
reach the bladder. Low frequencies (used for bladder) have poor resolution. 
The Solution: Variable Frequency Drive (VFA) 
● Hardware Upgrade: Replace the simple vibration motor with a Voice Coil Actuator 
(VCA) (e.g., Tectonic TEAX13 or Dayton Audio TT25) [],. 
● Why: A VCA works like a speaker. It can generate: 
○ 20-50 Hz (Rumble): For Bladder/Liver deep penetration. 
○ 200-800 Hz (Ping): For Bone Fracture resonance. 
● Dynamic Chirp: The device sends a "sweep" (chirp) from 20Hz to 1000Hz and analyzes 
which frequencies return, building a full Spectrogram of the tissue. 
Limitation C: Coupling Variability 
The Problem: If the probe is held at a slight angle, the contact area changes, altering the 
impedance reading,. 
The Solution: Impedance Head Design 
● Hardware: Use a Convex (Rounded) Silicone Tip rather than a flat one. This maintains 
constant contact area even if tilted up to 15 degrees. 
● Calibration: An "Air Check" calibration before every patient to zero out the probe's own 
mass. 
Limitation D: Differentiating Fluid from Fat 
The Problem: Ascites (fluid) and Adipose tissue (fat) are both "soft." A simple stiffness measure 
might confuse them. 
The Solution: Damping Ratio (\zeta) Analysis 
● Physics: Fluid has low stiffness and very low damping (it rings/splashes). Fat has low 
stiffness but high damping (it absorbs energy). 
● Algorithm: Measure the Q-Factor (sharpness) of the resonance peak. 
○ Sharp Peak (High Q): Fluid (Cyst/Ascites). 
○ Broad Peak (Low Q): Fat/Muscle.
