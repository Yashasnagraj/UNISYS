INTRODUCTION

Modern medical diagnostics relies heavily on imaging modalities such as X-ray, CT, MRI, and Ultrasound to visualize anatomical structures. While these technologies provide excellent spatial resolution, they share a critical limitation: they reveal structure but not function. A healed fracture may appear bridged on X-ray weeks before it can safely bear weight. A mass detected on ultrasound cannot be characterized as fluid-filled or solid without additional invasive testing. Furthermore, these technologies require expensive infrastructure, trained radiologists, and often expose patients to ionizing radiation.

ResoScan addresses this diagnostic gap by introducing a novel approach: Resonant Modal Spectroscopy (RMS) for non-invasive biomechanical tissue characterization. The device digitizes and quantifies the centuries-old clinical art of physical percussion and palpation. When a physician taps a patient's chest, they listen for "dullness" (fluid) versus "resonance" (air). When they palpate the abdomen, they feel for "stiffness" (mass) versus "softness" (normal). These subjective assessments, dependent on individual skill and experience, can now be transformed into objective, quantifiable metrics.

ResoScan employs a Voice Coil Actuator (VCA) to deliver controlled mechanical impulses across a programmable frequency range (20 Hz - 1 kHz). A high-bandwidth MEMS accelerometer captures the tissue's vibrational response. By analyzing the resonant frequencies, damping characteristics, and spectral signatures using Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) and machine learning algorithms, the device can differentiate between tissue states: fractured versus healed bone, air-filled versus consolidated lung, compliant versus stiff bladder.

This project proposes a low-cost, portable, radiation-free diagnostic platform with applications in:
1. Orthopedics - Monitoring fracture healing through stiffness recovery
2. Pulmonology - Detecting pneumothorax via acoustic hyper-resonance
3. Urology - Assessing bladder compliance through Lamb wave analysis

With an estimated Bill of Materials under $80 and a target retail price of $300-500, ResoScan democratizes functional diagnostics for resource-constrained settings, emergency triage, and home monitoring.

PROBLEM STATEMENT

The current landscape of medical diagnostics presents several critical challenges that ResoScan aims to address:

1. The Structure-Function Gap

X-ray imaging visualizes calcium deposition, which appears weeks after mechanical strength returns in healing fractures. Orthopedic surgeons currently rely on subjective clinical judgment to determine when a patient can safely bear weight or return to activity. Studies show that the mechanical stiffness of a fracture site recovers significantly earlier than radiological signs of healing. This gap leads to either:
- Premature mobilization causing re-fracture, or
- Unnecessarily prolonged immobilization causing muscle atrophy and joint stiffness

2. Subjectivity in Physical Examination

Physical percussion and palpation remain fundamental clinical skills, yet their accuracy varies dramatically with physician experience. Research indicates that manual percussion for ascites detection has sensitivity of only 83% and specificity of 56%. Junior clinicians often misinterpret findings, leading to delayed diagnoses or unnecessary imaging referrals.

3. Inaccessibility of Advanced Diagnostics

Current solutions for tissue stiffness assessment exist but remain inaccessible:
- FibroScan (liver elastography): $20,000 - $45,000, fails with ascites/obesity
- MyotonPRO (muscle diagnostics): $6,000 - $8,000, superficial tissues only
- Osstell (dental implants): $4,000, requires specialized hardware

There is no affordable, general-purpose handheld device for mechanical tissue characterization at the point of care.

4. Invasive Monitoring Procedures

Certain diagnostic assessments remain inherently invasive:
- Bladder compliance measurement requires urethral catheterization (infection risk)
- Fracture stability assessment sometimes requires surgical exploration
- Pneumothorax confirmation often requires CT imaging (radiation exposure)

5. The "Missing Middle" in Diagnostics

The current market offers either simple tools (tuning forks, reflex hammers - $15) or expensive imaging equipment ($20,000+). There is a distinct absence of intermediate diagnostic technology that provides quantitative functional assessment at an accessible price point.

ResoScan addresses these challenges by providing an objective, non-invasive, low-cost, portable device that quantifies tissue mechanical properties in real-time, bridging the gap between subjective clinical examination and expensive imaging infrastructure.

OBJECTIVES

1. Design and Fabricate a Handheld Prototype: Develop a compact, ergonomic device integrating a Voice Coil Actuator for mechanical excitation and MEMS accelerometer for vibration sensing, with total Bill of Materials under $100.

2. Implement Multi-Frequency Excitation: Enable programmable chirp signals (20 Hz - 1 kHz) to excite different tissue resonance modes, allowing characterization of both superficial structures (bone) and deep organs (bladder).

3. Develop Real-Time Signal Processing Pipeline: Create firmware for the ESP32 microcontroller to perform data acquisition, and companion software for FFT analysis, feature extraction, and spectral visualization.

4. Validate Resonance-Stiffness Correlation: Demonstrate that materials/tissues of different stiffness produce measurably different resonant frequency signatures, establishing the scientific foundation of the device.

5. Create Machine Learning Classification Models: Train classifiers to distinguish between tissue states (e.g., "bone-like" vs "soft tissue-like", "normal" vs "abnormal") based on spectral features.

6. Design Intuitive User Interface: Develop a visual dashboard displaying real-time FFT spectrograms, stiffness indices, and diagnostic indicators suitable for clinical interpretation.

7. Document Clinical Application Protocols: Establish standardized measurement procedures for each diagnostic application (fracture monitoring, pneumothorax screening, bladder assessment) including probe placement, preload requirements, and interpretation guidelines.

8. Ensure Reproducibility and Reliability: Implement force-feedback mechanisms and calibration protocols to minimize operator-dependent variability and soft tissue artifacts.

Signal Processing Workflow

1. Excitation Generation
- ESP32 DAC generates programmable waveforms:
  - Chirp: Linear frequency sweep 20 Hz to 1000 Hz over 500ms
  - Impulse: Short Gaussian pulse for transient response
  - Single Frequency: Sustained tone for specific resonance probing

2. Response Acquisition
- ADXL343 accelerometer samples at 3200 Hz (Nyquist: 1600 Hz)
- 12-bit resolution via I2C interface
- Continuous streaming to PC via USB Serial (115200 baud)

3. Spectral Analysis
- Windowed FFT (Hanning, 1024 points) for frequency resolution
- Power Spectral Density (PSD) calculation
- Peak detection for resonant frequency identification
- Damping ratio estimation via half-power bandwidth method

4. Feature Extraction
- f_resonant: Dominant resonant frequency (Hz)
- Amplitude: Peak magnitude at resonance
- Q-factor: Sharpness of resonance (stiffness indicator)
- Damping ratio: Energy dissipation (fluid indicator)
- Spectral centroid: Center of mass of spectrum

5. Classification
- Random Forest / SVM classifier trained on labeled data
- Binary output: "Normal" vs "Abnormal" or tissue type
- Confidence score for clinical interpretation

Clinical Application Protocols

Application 1: Fracture Healing Monitoring (Orthopedics)
- Measurement: Pitch-catch configuration
- Excitation: Medial malleolus (ankle)
- Sensing: Tibial tuberosity (below knee)
- Metric: Tibial Stiffness Index (TSI) = f2_injured / f2_healthy x 100%
- Interpretation: TSI > 80% indicates safe for weight-bearing

Application 2: Pneumothorax Detection (Pulmonology)
- Measurement: Direct percussion on chest wall
- Excitation: Intercostal space, mid-clavicular line
- Sensing: Same probe (impulse-response)
- Metric: Power ratio in 200-400 Hz band (left vs right)
- Interpretation: Ratio > 2.0 indicates hyper-resonance (air trapping)

Application 3: Bladder Compliance (Urology)
- Measurement: Lamb wave propagation
- Excitation: Suprapubic region (20-100 Hz)
- Sensing: 5-10 cm lateral displacement
- Metric: Wave velocity c_g (m/s)
- Interpretation: c_g > 4 m/s indicates elevated intravesical pressure

CONCLUSION

ResoScan represents a paradigm shift in medical diagnostics - from expensive, infrastructure-dependent imaging to affordable, portable, functional assessment. By digitizing the ancient art of percussion and palpation, we transform subjective clinical impressions into objective, quantifiable data.

The device addresses a critical unmet need in healthcare: the "Missing Middle" between simple physical examination tools and expensive imaging equipment. With applications spanning orthopedics (fracture healing), pulmonology (pneumothorax detection), and urology (bladder compliance), ResoScan offers a versatile platform for multi-modal diagnostics.

Our technical approach leverages established physics - resonant frequency analysis and mechanical impedance - validated by existing commercial devices (Osstell, MyotonPRO, FibroScan) in specialized domains. ResoScan's innovation lies in generalizing these principles into a single, affordable, handheld platform suitable for point-of-care use.

The prototype demonstrates feasibility with a BOM under $80, utilizing readily available components (ESP32, MEMS accelerometers, voice coil actuators). Real-time FFT analysis and machine learning classification provide immediate diagnostic feedback, potentially revolutionizing triage in emergency settings, remote healthcare, and home monitoring.

Future development will focus on:
- Clinical validation through IRB-approved trials
- Regulatory pathway (FDA Class II / De Novo)
- Miniaturization and integration into a single handheld unit
- Expansion of ML models with clinical datasets
- Development of condition-specific diagnostic algorithms

ResoScan envisions a future where every clinic, ambulance, and home has access to functional diagnostic technology - where a paramedic can detect pneumothorax in minutes, a sports physician can clear an athlete based on objective stiffness data, and a patient with neurogenic bladder can monitor their condition without catheterization.

By bridging the gap between the art of medicine and the science of measurement, ResoScan aims to democratize diagnostics and improve patient outcomes globally.
